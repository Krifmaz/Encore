1The big picture

Every recommendation in Encore, from pressing Shuffle to a “For you” mix, goes through the same four steps.

In plain words

  1. Signals. Encore notices what you play, like, put on playlists, skip and dislike.
  2. Profile. Each signal is credited not just to the song but also to its artists, its genres and its decade. Added up, that gives a number between −1 (you avoid it) and +1 (you love it) for every artist, genre and decade you've touched. Encore also knows which artists people on your server tend to play together.
  3. Score. Every song in the library gets a score from those numbers. Songs you heard in the last few hours, or skipped lately, lose points for a while.
  4. Queue. Songs are picked at random, but a high score makes a song more likely to be picked. Songs you've disliked are never picked. Finally the list is reordered so the same artist doesn't come up again within the next three songs.
Why random at all? If Encore always played the highest scores it would play the same 50 songs forever. A weighted draw means favourites come up more often, but any song you haven't ruled out can still turn up. That's how you rediscover things.
Brand new listener? With no plays, likes, skips or dislikes yet, every song scores the same and smart shuffle is simply a plain shuffle (still with artists spread out). It starts adapting from the first counted play.

2What Encore pays attention to

Seven kinds of signal. Each has a weight (how much it says about your taste) and a lifetime.

When is it a play, and when a skip?

  • A song counts as played once you've heard half of it or 4 minutes, whichever comes first (the same rule Last.fm uses). Songs under 30 seconds count at the end.
  • When a counted song ends or you move on, the app tells Encore how far it got: a song heard to the end counts half again as much as one left right after the halfway mark.
  • Pressing Next after the first 2 seconds but before the play counted is a skip. The later in the song, the less it counts: a skip just before the halfway mark is a fifth of one in the first seconds. Songs ending on their own are never skips, and Next on a disliked song isn't sent as a skip (the dislike already says more).
  • Encore reads your plays of the last 400 days (up to 20,000 of the newest) and your skips of the last 90 days. Older skips are deleted.

Dislikes and likes cancel each other

Disliking a song removes it from Liked Songs; liking a disliked song takes the dislike back. Both kinds of dislike can be undone at any time, and the song or artist immediately becomes playable again.

3Memory fades

Taste drifts. A play from last week says more about you than one from last year, so every play and skip counts less as it gets older.

Example: a play

You played a song 30 days ago. It still adds +0.84 instead of +1.0. A year-old play adds only +0.12.

Example: a skip

You skipped a song 7 days ago. It takes off 0.6 × 79% = −0.48. Three weeks later the same skip is only worth −0.15, and after 90 days it's gone.

Likes never fade

A song in Liked Songs is always worth +4, however long ago you liked it.

4Your taste profile

Each signal is credited to four places at once: the song, its artists, its genres and its decade.

Sharing out means a song tagged with five genres doesn't count five times as much as a song with one. Songs with no genre tag borrow the two genres their main artist's other songs use most, so they still land in the right genre mixes.

Squeezing totals into −1 … +1

After adding everything up, one favourite artist might have a total of 200 while others have 3. To stop one giant from drowning everyone else out, totals are squeezed onto a −1 … +1 scale:

affinity = tanh( 1.5 × total ÷ typical ) typical = the average of your ten biggest positive totals (at least 1) tanh = a smooth curve that flattens out near −1 and +1

A song's own total is squeezed with a fixed scale instead: tanh(total ÷ 3). So one like (+4) already makes a song's own value +0.87.

5Co-listening: artists people play together

Encore learns which artists belong together from how people on your server actually listen. With one listener it learns from your own sessions; with several it becomes a small “people who play X also play Y”.

Each play is linked to the three songs before it in the same session: the one right before counts 1, two back ½, three back ⅓. The counts for every pair of artists, across everyone's plays of the last year, become a similarity between 0 and 1:

similarity(X, Y) = together ÷ √(plays of X × plays of Y) × together ÷ (together + 1) the second part damps pairs heard together only once or twice
Example Radiohead was played 20 times, Massive Attack 10 times, and the “together” count is 6. Similarity = 6 ÷ √200 × 6 ÷ 7 = 0.42 × 0.86 = 0.36. Each artist keeps its 40 closest neighbours. This is rebuilt at most every 10 minutes.

How it reaches your profile

For every artist you like, Encore adds your liking × their similarity to each neighbour, then divides by the biggest result so the top neighbour is 1. That's the co-listening value in the score: it lets an artist you've never played come up because it sits next to artists you love.

In Sam's example we suppose the server has learned: Radiohead–Massive Attack 0.40, Portishead–Massive Attack 0.55, Radiohead–Muse 0.30, Radiohead–Portishead 0.20. Massive Attack gets 0.98 × 0.40 + 0.66 × 0.55 = {{MA_NEAR_RAW}}, the highest, so its co-listening value is 1.00; Muse gets {{MUSE_NEAR}}.

6Scoring a song

Every song gets one number, roughly between −1 and +1. Higher means a better fit for you right now.

score = 0.30 × artist average affinity of the song's artists + 0.25 × genre average affinity of its genres + 0.08 × era affinity of its decade + 0.17 × co-listening best co-listening value among its artists + 0.20 × the song its own history (plays, like, playlists, skips) − just-played penalty − 0.25 × recent skips the faded skip count

8Picking the queue

Step 1: the weighted draw

Each allowed song gets a weight from its score. Then songs are drawn one after another, without repeats, each time with a chance proportional to its weight.

weight = e score ÷ 0.3 0.3 is the “temperature”: lower follows the scores more strictly, higher is closer to a plain shuffle
{{TEMP_ROWS}}
ScoreWeight (vs. a neutral song)

A song scoring +0.9 is about 20 times as likely to be picked as a song you've never touched (score 0), and a song at −0.5 about 5 times less likely. Nothing allowed ever has zero chance.

Technically this is the Efraimidis–Spirakis method (equivalently “Gumbel top-k”): each song gets a random key from its weight and the best keys win, which is the same as drawing one by one.

Sam's first pick

{{SHARE_CHART}}

The chance each song is picked first out of Sam's seven allowed songs. A real library has thousands of songs, so each one's share is much smaller, but the ratios are the same: “Paranoid Android” is 12× as likely as Miles Davis, and Miles Davis is still 7× as likely as the twice-skipped Taylor Swift song.

Step 2: spreading artists out

The draw gives an order with favourites near the front, which could put three Radiohead songs in a row. Encore then walks down that order and, for each place, takes the first song that breaks none of these rules:

  1. Its main artist isn't one of the last three songs' main artists (so an artist comes back at most once every four songs).
  2. No artist, and no album, takes more than an eighth of the list (at least 2 songs each; 6 in a 50-song list).

If nothing in the next 400 songs fits, rule 2 is relaxed first, then rule 1, so a list made of only one artist still fills up.

A tiny 8-song example (R = Radiohead, MA = Massive Attack, P = Portishead, MD = Miles Davis; R1, R2 and R4 are on the same album). Radiohead is spaced out every four songs. At the end only Radiohead is left and Radiohead already has its 2-song share, so both rules are eased and R3, R4 (dashed) fill the last places. With a real library there are almost always other artists to pick, so this rarely happens.

9Every feature, and how it uses the same engine

All features share one profile and one scoring formula. They differ only in which songs are in the running and how strictly the score is followed.

Smart shuffle and “keep going”

On by default (Settings → Playback → Smart shuffle). The Shuffle button asks for 50 songs from the whole library, drawn as in section 8. When your queue runs out, Encore keeps the music going with another 50 the same way, leaving out every song already played or queued this session. If you switch smart shuffle off, both become a plain random shuffle (dislikes still left out).

Shuffling a playlist or album

Turning shuffle on for a list first shuffles it on your device instantly, then asks the server for a taste-weighted order of the same songs: favourites tend to come earlier, the same artist is spread out, disliked songs drop out. If the server can't answer, or the queue changed meanwhile, the plain shuffle stays.

Genre shuffle

Pick one or more genres (your favourite genres are listed first, each with its song count; genres with fewer than 3 songs aren't listed). Encore draws 100 songs that carry any of the ticked genres, weighted by taste. When the queue runs out, “keep going” sticks to the same genres until you play something else. Untagged songs count under their artist's usual genres.

Each mix shows 30 songs on the page; pressing play makes a fresh 100-song version. Mixes with fewer than 5 songs are hidden. Every refresh is a new draw, so mixes change each visit.

Start radio (“More like this”)

Starts with the song you picked, followed by 50 songs like it. Here the main ingredient is how similar each song is to the seed, with your taste as a nudge:

similarity = 0.45 × artist same artist 1, otherwise their co-listening similarity + 0.40 × genres shared genres ÷ all genres of both + 0.15 × era 1 − decades apart ÷ 30 years (0.3 if unknown) candidate = similarity + 0.25 × your score

Songs with similarity 0.05 or less are left out. The best 400 candidates go into a much stricter draw (temperature 0.12 instead of 0.3) so the radio stays on topic, and the seed's own artist may fill at most a quarter of the list (12 of 50): the point is to find other artists like it.
